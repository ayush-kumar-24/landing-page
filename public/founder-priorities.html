Founder Priorities

What should a founder focus on? Not everything.

Founders rarely suffer from a shortage of things to do. The harder problem is deciding which one deserves attention now — and which important-looking work can wait.

Quick answer

A founder should focus on the priority most likely to change the business's next important outcome.

That priority depends on four pieces of context: where the startup is, what is constraining progress, what the evidence says, and where founder attention has the most leverage.

The answer is therefore rarely a universal list like “sales first” or “product first.” A useful priority is specific to the current situation and clear enough that the team knows what can wait.

Why prioritisation feels hard

Founders do not choose between good and bad ideas. They choose between several plausible priorities.

01 · Urgency

The loudest thing wins.

Slack messages, customer requests and deadlines can consume attention even when another issue matters more to the next business outcome.

02 · Visibility

Visible work feels safer.

Shipping features, redesigning pages or creating campaigns can feel more concrete than investigating a difficult uncertainty.

03 · Breadth

Every weakness becomes a project.

A business can have many imperfect areas. Trying to improve all of them at once dilutes focus and makes progress harder to attribute.

04 · Founder gravity

Everything routes back to one person.

When the founder is the default owner of too many decisions, prioritisation becomes a personal workload problem instead of a business sequence.

Five filters for founder priorities

Before asking “What should I do?”, ask what should decide?

A priority becomes clearer when competing tasks are judged against the same context instead of against urgency, enthusiasm or whoever asked last.

01

Outcome

What important result needs to move next — validation, activation, retention, revenue, runway, delivery or decision speed?

02

Constraint

What currently limits that outcome? Work outside the constraint may improve a local metric without changing the business result.

03

Evidence

What do customer behaviour, usage, sales, financials or operating signals actually support — and what is still assumption?

04

Leverage

Which move could unlock several downstream problems, reduce uncertainty or create a decision that changes what happens next?

05

Founder role

Does this require the founder's judgment, context or authority — or is founder attention being used on work that can be owned elsewhere?

Founder priorities by startup stage

The questions change as the startup changes.

Stage is useful context, not a rigid checklist. The current bottleneck can still override the usual pattern.

Idea / validation

Is the problem worth solving?

Attention often belongs on evidence that a specific customer has a meaningful problem and will take action to solve it.

  • Customer problem evidence
  • Narrow audience
  • Willingness to commit
MVP / early use

Can users reach the value?

Once something exists, the priority often shifts from ideas to whether people can complete the core workflow and experience the promised value.

  • Activation
  • Onboarding friction
  • Core usage behaviour
Early traction

Does value repeat?

With initial users or revenue, the next question is often whether customers return, pay, refer or show enough pull to support repeatable growth.

  • Retention
  • Pricing and value
  • Repeatable demand
Growth / scale

Can the system carry more?

As demand becomes more reliable, priorities can move toward economics, capacity, ownership, systems and reducing founder dependency.

  • Unit economics
  • Team ownership
  • Operating capacity

A founder prioritisation framework

From everything important to one clear next focus.

This is not a scoring trick for making uncertainty disappear. It is a sequence for making the trade-off explicit and testable.

Useful rule: choose a primary priority because of the outcome it changes, then let supporting tasks serve that priority instead of competing with it.
01

Name the next outcome.

What must become true next for the business to move: stronger validation, activation, retention, revenue, runway, delivery or ownership?

02

Find what is blocking it.

Identify the bottleneck or uncertainty that currently limits that outcome. If the constraint is unclear, diagnose before committing more resources.

03

List the competing moves.

Write the real alternatives. “Do everything” is not an option; each move consumes time, money, attention or organisational capacity.

04

Compare impact with evidence.

Prefer moves that plausibly affect the constraint and are supported by enough evidence to justify the cost — or efficiently produce the evidence you are missing.

05

Choose the smallest decisive action.

Turn the priority into a concrete next move with a result you can observe, then reassess instead of locking the business into a permanent plan.

Priority vs urgency

Not every urgent task deserves founder-level focus.

Urgent work

Time-sensitive because something is due, broken or waiting.

Urgent work may need action, but timing alone does not tell you whether it is the highest-leverage use of founder attention.

  • Deadline today
  • Customer escalation
  • Operational incident
  • Approval waiting
Strategic priority

Important because it changes the next meaningful outcome.

A priority connects attention to the current constraint or the most important uncertainty — and gives the rest of the team a clearer sequence.

  • Resolve the growth bottleneck
  • Validate the riskiest assumption
  • Fix the limiting workflow
  • Create ownership that removes founder dependency

Founder Priorities FAQ

Questions founders ask when everything feels important.

What should a startup founder focus on?
A founder should focus on the priority most likely to change the business's next important outcome. That depends on the startup's stage, the current constraint, the available evidence and where founder attention has the most leverage.
How do founders decide what to prioritize?
Start by defining the outcome that matters now, identify what is constraining it, compare possible actions by expected impact and evidence, then choose the smallest high-leverage move that can test or improve that constraint.
How many priorities should a founder have?
A founder may have many responsibilities, but it is useful to distinguish one primary business priority from supporting work. If several initiatives compete equally for attention, the team can lose clarity about what outcome matters most.
Do founder priorities change by startup stage?
Yes. The questions that matter often change as a startup moves from problem validation to product use, retention, repeatable demand and then scaling. Stage is context, not a rigid rule; the current constraint still matters.
What is the difference between a priority and an urgent task?
An urgent task demands attention because of timing. A priority deserves attention because it materially affects an important outcome. Some work is both urgent and important, but urgency alone does not make something the highest-leverage priority.
